# TikTok & Instagram content plan

Distribution is the real job. The research behind this plan (*Indie mobile app money playbook*) found that almost every successful small app uses one of three engines; this plan runs the cheapest one first:

1. **Owned slideshow accounts** (now): find hooks and formats that work, for free.
2. **Paid micro-creators** (after a format repeats): real servers and bartenders posting about the app.
3. **Apple Search Ads** (after launch): a few exact keywords, small daily budget.

## Ground rules

These keep the accounts alive and keep you within FTC and platform rules.

- **Never invent stories or numbers and present them as real.** No fake “I made $X” confessions, fake reviews or fake testimonials. Real numbers come from your testers, with their permission, or are clearly labelled as an example.
- **Memes and POV jokes are fine.** Everyone reads them as humor, not as factual claims.
- **Original posts only.** TikTok and Instagram now demote reposted, near-duplicate and templated content (TikTok since Sept 2025; Instagram has extended this to carousels in 2026). Run one account per platform, vary every post, and don’t copy the same slides between accounts unchanged.
- **Label AI images** where the platform asks (TikTok requires a label on realistic AI content).
- **No bought followers, likes or comments.** No bot accounts.
- **Paid creators must disclose.** Every paid post carries #ad or the platform’s paid-partnership label (FTC endorsement rules; penalties can exceed $50,000 per violation).

## Account setup (day 0)

- **Handle:** `@[brand]app` or `@[brand]tips` on both TikTok and Instagram.
- **Profile picture:** the app icon.
- **Bio:** “Tip tracker for servers & bartenders. No ads, ever. 👇”
- **Link in bio:**
  - Until launch: a simple waitlist or landing page, or a TestFlight public link once the Apple account exists.
  - After launch: the App Store and Google Play links.
- **Account type:** TikTok Business and Instagram Professional, for free analytics. On TikTok, only use commercially licensed sounds from the Commercial Music Library.

## Warm-up (days 1–14)

The research’s strongest zero-budget case (Parceladinho, $1.6K MRR in 23 days) spent **two weeks warming up the account before posting**.

1. Spend 20–30 minutes a day on each platform, as a real person would. Follow 50–100 server, bartender and restaurant creators, and watch their posts to the end.
2. Like and leave genuine, useful comments. Answer tip-out and tax questions where you can. A budgeting app gained 140 customers in 30 days from value-adding comments alone.
3. Save the posts in the niche that hit 100K+ views. Note their hook (first slide or first 2 seconds), how many slides they use, the text style and the sound.
4. Days 8–14: post once a day, value or humor posts only, with no app mention.

## The format: 6-slide carousels

Use TikTok photo mode and Instagram carousels.

| Slide | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook:** big text over a photo. One idea, readable in 2 seconds. |
| 2–5 | **The payoff:** numbers, steps or the joke, one point per slide |
| 6 | **Soft close:** a takeaway, a question to the viewer, or “I track mine in [Brand]” |

Practical details:
- Put the app on slide 6 at most, and only on about half the posts. Value posts get the caption line “Link in bio if you want a tip tracker with no ads.”
- On TikTok, always add a trending sound.
- Images: your own photos of restaurant scenes (no customers’ faces), stock photos, or labelled AI images. Text in large white type with a dark outline, 3–8 words per slide.

Hook formulas that performed in the research:
- **Doubt flipped by data:** “[Person] + [doubt] → saw the numbers → changed their mind”. This reached 50–147K views where feature hooks got under 3K.
- **Confession, when it’s real:** “I didn’t know I had to report tips by the 10th…”
- **Number shock:** “A $200 night is really $___/hr.”
- **Save-worthy list:** “What to keep for tax time if you work for tips (save this).”

## Measure and decide

Keep a simple sheet with one row per post:

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-11-12 |
| Platform | TikTok |
| Hook # | 9 |
| Hook text | The $20 rule nobody told you about |
| Views (48h) | 12,400 |
| Saves / shares | 310 / 95 |
| Profile visits | 180 |
| Link clicks | 41 |
| Notes | Trending sound X |

Rules from the research:
- **Kill** a hook that stays under about 1,000 views after 48 hours. Don’t repost it.
- **Iterate** on anything over about 10K: post 3 variations (new image, new first line, same structure).
- **Double down** on anything over about 50K. It becomes a recurring series.
- Expect **30–100 posts before a format clearly works**. One founder said it plainly: the first month is research, not results.
- **Diagnose:** high views but low clicks means fix the last slide and caption; low views but good click rate means fix the hook.
- Once the app is live, App Store Connect (*Sources*) and Play Console show installs per day. Line them up with your post dates.

## Phase 2: paid micro-creators (after a format repeats)

When one format hits 50K+ at least twice, pay real servers and bartenders to make their own version.

- **Find them** by searching #serverlife and #bartenderlife for creators with 2K–50K followers whose posts get good engagement.
- **Pay:** $50–150 per video to start, or a base plus $1–3 per 1,000 views. The research found returns are very uneven (in one program the top 1% of posts drove about 88% of views), so test many small creators, not one big one.
- **Contract terms:** one trackable link or code per creator, the right to reuse their video as an ad, and mandatory #ad / paid-partnership label.
- **Don’t start with big influencers.** One app paid “thousands per video” to large creators before finding a format and cut its margin to 5–10%.

**Outreach message (DM):**

> Hi [name], I loved your post about [specific post]. I’m building [Brand], a tip tracker for servers with no ads and no account. Would you be up for making one post in your own style about how you track tips? I can pay $[amount] for one video, plus a bonus if it passes [X] views. It would be labeled as a paid partnership. Happy to send the app first so you can see if you’d actually use it.

## Phase 3: Apple Search Ads (after launch)

The only paid channel the research found consistently profitable for small indie apps:
- Run one country per campaign, with exact-match keywords only and Search Match off.
- Start with your brand name plus 3–10 keywords (“tip tracker”, “server tips”, “tip log”, “bartender tips”) at $10–40 a day.
- Check after 2–3 weeks. My rule of thumb, not a sourced benchmark: keep keywords whose cost per trial is below about a third of a year’s subscription, and pause the rest.
