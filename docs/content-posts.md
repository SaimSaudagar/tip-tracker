## Tax

### 7. No tax on tips is real (2025–2028). 3 things servers get wrong
1. "No tax on tips" is real, but it's not what most people think.
2. It's a federal income-tax deduction of up to $25,000 of tips a year, for 2025 through 2028.
3. ❌ It's not "no tax": you still pay Social Security and Medicare on tips, and your state may still tax them.
4. ❌ Auto-gratuity and mandatory service charges don't count, only tips the customer chose to give.
5. ❌ It shrinks for incomes over $150,000 ($300,000 married filing jointly).
6. Keep a record of every tip. Save this for tax time. (Source: IRS, One Big Beautiful Bill: no tax on tips)

Caption: "Did you know about the service charge rule? Not tax advice, check IRS.gov."

### 8. Auto-gratuity is NOT a tip for the new deduction
1. That 18% auto-grat on the big party? It's not a "tip" for the new deduction.
2. Tips have to be voluntary: the guest chooses whether and how much.
3. An automatic service charge is set by the restaurant, so the IRS treats it as wages, not tips.
4. So it doesn't count toward the up-to-$25,000 tip deduction.
5. Keep service charges separate from tips in your records.
6. (Tipbook keeps them on a separate line for exactly this.)

Caption: "Does your restaurant add auto-grat? Not tax advice. Source: IRS.gov"

### 10. Your tip report is due by the 10th
1. Your tip report is due by the 10th. Every month.
2. Made $20+ in tips from one job last month? Tell that employer by the 10th.
3. Many restaurants collect it in the POS when you clock out (tip declaration).
4. Otherwise: a written, signed report with your name, the month and your total tips.
5. Tips you don't report can mean owing Social Security and Medicare on them later, plus a possible penalty.
6. Set a reminder for the 8th. (Source: IRS Publication 531)

Caption: "Does your POS ask you to declare tips? 👇"

## Tip-outs

### 19. How much should you tip out the bar? · Example
1. How much should you tip out the bar?
2. There's no one rule. It's set by each restaurant.
3. Common examples: about 1–2% of your sales, or a share of your alcohol sales.
4. Ask: is it % of sales, % of tips, or % of alcohol?
5. Then work out what it costs you on a slow night.
6. What does your place do? 👇

Caption: "Example ranges, every house is different."

### 20. 3% of sales vs 3% of tips: which costs you more? · Example
1. "3% tip-out" can mean two very different things.
2. Your night: $1,000 in sales, $200 in tips (example)
3. 3% of sales = $30
4. 3% of tips = $6
5. And on % of sales, a table that stiffs you still costs you.
6. Know which one your restaurant uses.

### 21. Tip pooling explained in 6 slides
1. Tip pooling, explained in 6 slides
2. A pool collects tips and splits them by hours, points or shifts.
3. Under federal law, managers and supervisors can't keep any of it.
4. If your employer pays a tipped wage (under $7.25), the pool can include only tipped staff: servers, bussers, bartenders, hosts.
5. If they pay full minimum wage, cooks and dishwashers can be included too.
6. Your state may have stricter rules. (Source: U.S. Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 15)
